Call for Tutorials

June 21–24, 2027 · Washington, DC, USA

Important Dates

  • Tutorial proposal submission deadline: February 21, 2027 AoE
  • Notification of acceptance: March 14, 2027 AoE
  • Camera-ready extended abstract due: April 7, 2027 AoE
  • Final tutorial materials due: May 12, 2027 AoE

Overview

ICHI 2027 invites proposals for tutorials that introduce attendees to cutting-edge methods, tools, and practices in computing-oriented health informatics. Led by domain experts, tutorials give researchers, practitioners, and students a focused opportunity to learn emerging techniques and see how they are applied in real health settings. All accepted tutorials will be featured in the main conference program.

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Large language models and generative AI for clinical and biomedical applications
  • Health data science and machine learning with EHR, imaging, omics, and sensor data
  • Interoperability and health data standards (e.g., FHIR, OMOP)
  • Privacy-preserving and federated learning
  • Fairness, transparency, and responsible AI in healthcare
  • Digital health, mobile health, and wearable technologies
  • Clinical decision support and implementation science

Tutorials may target any level, from introductory to advanced, but should be well scoped, engaging, and clearly matched to their intended audience.

Tutorial Format

Tutorials can be half-day (3 hours) or full-day (6 hours) in length. Instructors must commit to preparing high-quality tutorial materials (e.g., slides, code notebooks, datasets, or exercises) that reflect the standards of ICHI. Where appropriate, tutorials may include interactive components such as live demonstrations, hands-on exercises, or group discussions. Any data used in hands-on activities must be publicly available or appropriately de-identified.

Submission Guidelines

Submissions should be made via the ICHI 2027 OpenReview system under the “Tutorial Proposals” track. Proposals must follow the IEEE Proceedings Format for LaTeX or Word using conference mode. Each proposal should be submitted as a single PDF consisting of the two parts described below.

The Tutorial Proposals submission link will be posted here when available.

Part 1: Extended Abstract (1 Page)

A one-page extended abstract that will be published in the conference proceedings if the tutorial is accepted. It must include:

  • Title: The title of the tutorial
  • Instructors: Name(s), affiliation(s), and email address(es) of the instructor(s)
  • Abstract: A summary of the tutorial’s scope, main topics, and motivation
  • Intended Audience: Who will benefit from the tutorial and its level (beginner, intermediate, or advanced)
  • Learning Outcomes: The knowledge, skills, and new perspectives attendees will gain
  • Instructor Biographies: A short biography of each instructor

Part 2: Full Tutorial Proposal (Maximum 4 Pages)

The full proposal expands on the extended abstract and is used by the Tutorial Committee to evaluate the submission. It should include:

  • Outline and Schedule: A breakdown of the main topics with an approximate timeline for each session.
  • Learning Objectives: Clear, actionable objectives describing what attendees will be able to do by the end of the tutorial.
  • Tutorial Materials: The materials attendees will receive (e.g., slides, code notebooks, datasets, reading lists) and how they will be shared.
  • Case Studies (Optional): Real-world systems, clinical deployments, practical experiences, or case studies to be discussed.
  • Instructor Expertise: Short CVs for all instructors, highlighting relevant expertise and prior teaching or tutorial experience.
  • Logistics: Preferred length (half-day or full-day), expected number of attendees, and any technical requirements (e.g., Wi-Fi, attendee laptops, cloud computing resources).
  • Additional Information (Optional): Anything else that would help the Tutorial Committee evaluate the proposal, such as previous offerings of the tutorial, past attendance, or links to sample slides.

Review Process

Proposals will be reviewed by the Tutorial Committee based on the following criteria:

  • Relevance and Interest: Relevance to ICHI and anticipated interest among attendees
  • Timeliness: Novelty and timeliness of the topic
  • Content and Scope: Depth, breadth, and organization of the topics, and fit with the proposed length
  • Learning Design: Clarity of the learning objectives and effectiveness of the planned delivery format
  • Materials: Quality and completeness of the planned materials
  • Instructor Qualifications: Expertise, credentials, and teaching track record of the instructor(s)
